# Gordon Ramsay Techniques Reference
*Excerpted from Gordon Ramsay MasterClass Workbook*

## Core Philosophy
- "When you cook, it's not from a script — it's from the heart."
- "You don't need a $100,000 kitchen to cook good food."
- Never waste ingredients — use every part (carcasses for stock, herb tops for salads)
- Buy seasonally. Shop local farmers markets when possible.

## Essential Techniques

### Eggs
- **Poached**: Use white wine vinegar in water. Stir water to swirling before dropping egg in from a small bowl. 2 min for set whites. Season after removing.
- **Scrambled**: Do NOT beat or season before cooking. Add eggs + butter cold to pan. Stir constantly with rubber spatula on medium heat. Alternate on/off heat. Stop before fully set — residual heat finishes them. Fold in crème fraîche at end.

### Chicken
- Never cook chicken straight from the fridge — let it come to room temperature
- Sear skin-side down first; don't move until skin releases and browns (~4 min)
- Baste continuously with butter as it cooks
- Baste pan drippings into jus/sauce using deglaze technique (brandy or apple juice)

### Fish
- "The skin is your safety net" — always cook salmon skin-side down first
- Score skin before cooking to prevent curling
- Wipe knife clean between every cut when filleting
- Never leave scallops in water — they won't develop color

### Pasta
- Fresh pasta uses 00 flour + whole eggs + yolks + olive oil
- Knead 10 minutes until firm and elastic; rest 20 min wrapped in plastic
- "You can always add flour, but you can't take it away"
- Salt pasta water generously — it should taste of the sea
- Cook to al dente; rinse with cold water to stop cooking and remove starch

### Searing / Maillard Reaction
- Get pan fully hot before adding oil or protein
- Pat proteins dry before searing for maximum browning
- Don't crowd the pan — steam prevents the Maillard reaction
- The brown bits (fond) left in the pan are pure flavor — deglaze and use them

### Knife Skills
- Three-finger rule: middle finger in front, two behind when slicing
- Relax hands — grip should feel like holding a steering wheel
- Keep knives sharp; a dull knife is more dangerous than a sharp one
- Don't over-chop herbs — you lose flavor and scent to the board

### Vegetables
- "Ugly vegetables have more flavor"
- Buy by season for best flavor and price
- Blanch then shock in ice water to preserve color and stop cooking
- Roast on herb beds (rosemary, thyme, parsley) for aromatic infusion

### Sauce Fundamentals
- Build sauces from fond: deglaze with alcohol → add stock → reduce → strain
- Add butter at the end of a sauce off heat for gloss and richness
- Taste and adjust salt at every stage

### Seasoning
- Season in layers throughout cooking, not just at the end
- Use finishing salt (e.g., Maldon flakes) for texture contrast at the plate
- Acid (lemon juice, vinegar) brightens and lifts finished dishes

## Kitchen Essentials Brian Should Have
- Cast iron skillet (for searing)
- Nonstick pan (for eggs, delicate fish)
- Heavy saucepan
- Sharp chef's knife + paring knife
- Large wooden cutting board (24"×18" min)
- Microplane grater
- Strainer/sieve
- Aluminum foil, parchment paper, plastic wrap
- Finishing salt (Maldon)

## Beef Wellington (Advanced Reference)
1. Sear seasoned beef tenderloin in cast iron; brush with Dijon mustard while hot
2. Layer crepe → prosciutto → mushroom duxelle; roll beef inside using plastic wrap
3. Wrap in puff pastry, seal, score decoratively, egg wash
4. Bake at 425°F convection for 18–20 min until golden; rest 5 min before slicing

## Lobster Ravioli (Advanced Reference)
- Filling: ground salmon + egg white mousse + lobster meat + basil + lemon
- Sauce: reduce lobster stock with cream
- Tomato chutney: stew blanched, peeled, diced plum tomatoes with basil 20 min
- Poach ravioli in lobster stock 90 seconds; toss in lemon vinaigrette
